Simple Sourdough — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 13 October 2026

The short version

Simple Sourdough keeps your bakes, journal, photos and settings on your phone. The developer collects nothing. The app goes online for only two things: an optional local weather reading, which is off unless you turn it on, and the one-time unlock purchase, which Google Play handles. Neither involves an account, and nothing is sent to the developer.

What the app stores, and where

Your starter schedule, bake progress, settings, and bake journal (including any photos you attach to journal entries) are saved only in the app's private storage on your device. Photos you take for the journal are copied into that private storage. The app also keeps the last weather reading (if you use weather), a count of your bakes, and whether you have unlocked unlimited bakes. None of this is uploaded, synced, or shared with the developer or anyone else.

Optional local weather

Temperature and humidity affect how fast dough rises, so the app can show the current conditions where you are. This is off unless you turn it on. When you do, the app asks for your approximate (coarse) location, never your precise location. It rounds the coordinates to about 1 km and sends them to Open-Meteo, a free weather service, to get the current temperature and humidity.

The app sends no account, name, or other identifier with this request. Like any website or web service, Open-Meteo can see your phone's IP address when the request arrives. The latest reading is saved on your phone so the app does not have to ask again every time you open it.

To stop this, turn weather off in the app or deny or remove the location permission in your phone's settings. The app then makes no weather requests at all, and turning weather off also deletes the saved reading.

The location is only read while the app is open, never in the background, and the app never keeps your location itself, only the temperature and humidity reading.

The one-time purchase

Your first 2 guided bakes are free. Unlimited bakes need a one-time in-app purchase, which Google Play processes from start to finish. The developer never sees your card or payment details. Google's privacy policy covers the payment. Each time the app opens, it asks Google Play whether your account already owns the unlock, so it comes back by itself after a reinstall or on a new phone. On your phone, the app stores only a flag saying whether you have unlocked unlimited bakes and a count of your bakes.

What the app does not do

  • No accounts, sign-in, or personal information requested
  • No internet use apart from the optional weather request and Google Play purchases
  • No analytics, tracking, advertising, or crash reporting of its own
  • No data sold, and nothing shared with anyone beyond the weather request described above and what Google Play needs to process the purchase

Permissions the app uses

Notifications and exact alarms are used so bake timers can buzz you when a step finishes, including when the phone is locked, and for a weekly reminder to feed your starter (Sundays at 9:00 am unless you change or switch it off on the Starter tab). While "Ring timers as alarms" is on (it is by default, and you can switch to quiet notifications), the app can also show a full-screen alarm and run a short foreground service so the timer keeps ringing until you stop it.

Approximate location is requested only if you turn on local weather, and is used only for the weather request described above.

Internet access, network state and Google Play billing are used only for the weather request and for Google Play purchases.

Camera access happens only through your phone's own camera app when you choose to add a photo to a journal entry. Simple Sourdough itself does not request the camera permission.

Backups

If you make a backup, the app creates a file that you save or share yourself, wherever you choose. The app does not upload backups anywhere, and the developer never receives them.

Separately, if your phone backs up its apps to your Google account, Android includes the journal, settings and starter schedule in that backup, but not the photos. That backup belongs to your Google account; the developer cannot see it.

Deleting your data

Uninstalling the app permanently deletes everything it stored on your phone. Backup files you saved or shared elsewhere stay where you put them until you delete them yourself. If your phone backs up to your Google account (see Backups), that copy stays with your Google account and comes back if you reinstall, until you remove it in your Google account's backup settings.

Children

The app is suitable for general audiences. The developer collects no data from anyone, children included.
